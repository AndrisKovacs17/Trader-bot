\section{The Kalman Filter and Its Extensions}
\label{sec:kalman}

The Kalman filter~\cite{kalman1960new} is the optimal Bayesian estimator for
linear dynamical systems under additive Gaussian noise. The state update is:
\begin{equation}
    \hat{x}_{t|t} = \hat{x}_{t|t-1} + K_t (y_t - H \hat{x}_{t|t-1}),
    \label{eq:kalman_update}
\end{equation}
where the gain $K_t$ is determined by the ratio of prediction and measurement
covariance:
\begin{equation}
    K_t = P_{t|t-1} H^\top (H P_{t|t-1} H^\top + R)^{-1}.
    \label{eq:kalman_gain}
\end{equation}
When $R \to \infty$, $K \to 0$: the filter distrusts the measurement.
When $R \to 0$, $K \to 1$: the measurement nearly overwrites the state.
This simple rule is the core intuition behind KCMamba.

\subsection{Learned Kalman Filters}

In the neural Kalman literature, the matrices $F, H, Q, R$ are parameterised
by networks~\cite{krishnan2015deep,fraccaro2017disentangled}.
The \emph{Deep Kalman Filter}~\cite{krishnan2015deep} allows non-linear
transitions; the \emph{Kalman Variational Autoencoder}~\cite{fraccaro2017disentangled}
learns disentangled representations of latent states.
KCMamba builds on these ideas but operates as a sequence forecaster rather
than as an autoencoder.

\section{State Space Models}
\label{sec:ssm}

\subsection{S4}

S4~\cite{gu2021s4} models a $d$-dimensional input sequence via an
$N$-dimensional latent dynamic:
\begin{align}
    h'(t) &= A h(t) + B u(t), \\
    y(t) &= C h(t).
\end{align}
After discretisation: $h_t = \bar{A} h_{t-1} + \bar{B} u_t$, $y_t = C h_t$,
where $\bar{A} = e^{\Delta A}$.

\subsection{Mamba}

The key innovation of Mamba~\cite{gu2023mamba} is input-dependent selectivity:
$B$, $C$, and $\Delta$ depend on the input $u_t$. This lets the model decide
what to remember and what to forget. A hardware-efficient parallel scan achieves
$O(T)$ memory and time complexity.

\subsection{Fair Mamba}

The comparison baseline, referred to as ``Fair Mamba'', is a channel-wise Mamba
with identical hyperparameters and training protocol as KCMamba. The ``fair''
label means the parameter budget and data are identical, so any difference in
results comes solely from the architecture.

\section{Long-Term Time Series Forecasting}
\label{sec:ltsf}

\subsection{Problem Formulation}

Given $\mathbf{X} = (x_1, \ldots, x_L) \in \mathbb{R}^{L \times d}$, the goal
is to predict $\hat{\mathbf{X}} = (\hat{x}_{L+1}, \ldots, \hat{x}_{L+H})$
by minimising normalised MSE.

\subsection{Reference Models}

\begin{itemize}
    \item \emph{Informer} reduces Transformer complexity to
          $O(L \log L)$ via ProbSparse
          attention~\cite{zhou2021informer}.
    \item \emph{Autoformer} applies series decomposition and
          auto-correlation-based attention~\cite{wu2021autoformer}.
    \item \emph{DLinear} achieves surprisingly strong results with a
          plain linear projection, questioning the necessity of complex
          architectures~\cite{zeng2023transformers} --- a reminder that a
          simpler model is not always worse.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Datasets}
\label{sec:datasets}

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Exchange-Rate}~\cite{lai2018modeling}: 8 currency exchange
          rates, 7\,588 daily samples. Its near-unit-root dynamics make the
          Kalman structure particularly effective.
    \item \textbf{ETTh1 / ETTh2}~\cite{zhou2021informer}: Hourly transformer-station
          electricity data ($d=7$, 17\,420 steps). ETTh1 is strongly periodic;
          ETTh2 is noisier.
    \item \textbf{Weather, ECL}: Not included in our experiments but are
          standard benchmarks in the LTSF literature.
\end{itemize}

\section{Noise Robustness}
\label{sec:robustness}

The noise sensitivity of time series models is rarely studied in
a systematic way. PatchTST~\cite{nie2022patchtst} indirectly improves noise
tolerance through patch tokenisation, but robustness is not its primary goal.
Augmentation-based noise robustness (training models on corrupted data)
has received little attention in the LTSF community.

In this work the evaluation protocol is:
$\tilde{x}_t = x_t + \varepsilon_t$,
$\varepsilon_t \sim \mathcal{N}(0, \sigma^2 I)$, $\sigma \in \{0, 1, 3, 5\}$.
Both training and test sets are corrupted with the same $\sigma$, so this
is uniform data-quality degradation, not a domain-shift scenario.
