\chapter{Conclusion}
\label{ch:osszegzes}

\section{Main Findings}

KCMamba integrates a differentiable Kalman filtering mechanism into the Mamba
SSM. The experiments on three datasets lead to the following conclusions:

The central finding concerns KCMamba's noise robustness.
Going from $\sigma=0$ to $\sigma=5$, KCMamba's average MSE degradation
is $+1\,528\%$, versus $+28\,234\%$ for Fair Mamba, an $18.5\times$
difference. In the Exchange/$s=16$ setting this becomes five orders of
magnitude: $+66\%$ vs.\ $+152\,350\%$.

The long-sequence sweep (Section~\ref{sec:longseq},
$T \in \{96,192,336,512\}$ on ETTm1 and Exchange) confirms KCMamba's
advantage at longer contexts. At $T{=}336$, $\sigma{=}1$: KCMamba MSE
$0.502$ vs.\ LSTM $0.735$ ($\mathbf{-31.7\%}$, the largest margin over
LSTM in the sweep). At $T{=}512$, $\sigma{=}3$ on Exchange: KCMamba
$0.773$, Fair Mamba $2.121$ ($\mathbf{-63.6\%}$, the largest margin over
Fair Mamba). On ETTm1 at $\sigma{=}3$ KCMamba is the only model whose
MSE monotonically decreases with sequence length
($0.673 \to 0.466$, $-31\%$, $T{:}\,96 \to 512$).

The stride $\times$ sequence-length sweep
(Section~\ref{sec:stride_longseq}, Exchange, $s \in \{1,16\}$,
$T \in \{192,336,512\}$) reveals two distinct patterns.
At $s{=}1$, $\sigma{=}3$, KCMamba's margin over LSTM grows monotonically
with $T$, reaching $+35\%$ at $T{=}512$ ($0.836$ vs.\ $1.292$).
At $s{=}16$, $\sigma{\geq}3$, Fair Mamba catastrophically collapses
(MSE $8.1$--$8.5$), a $7$--$9\times$ stability gap vs.\ KCMamba which
stays at $1.1$--$1.3$.

The model learned on its own to reduce $K$ as noise increases
($0.706 \to 0.073$), matching the behaviour expected from the explicit
Kalman formula. The architectural prior genuinely guides learning.
With stride $s=16$, the Kalman structure also acts as an implicit
regulariser that partially compensates for the lack of training windows.

\section{Limitations}

KCMamba does not win in every configuration, and the experimental
framework contains several simplifications that meaningfully restrict the
generalisability of our conclusions.

At $\sigma=0$, $s=16$ Fair Mamba nearly memorises and achieves a much better
MSE. This suggests that in noise-free settings the explicit Kalman structure
imposes unnecessary overhead: the always-present filtering term causes mild
underfitting, and the architecture cannot fully switch the filter off.

On ETTh1 at $s=1$, LSTM is the best, since its gating mechanism exploits the large
data volume. The forget/input gate pair realises a similar adaptive filter, but
more flexibly, because it is not tied to the $K = Q/(Q+R)$ structure. This is
not a KCMamba failure but illustrates that the utility of the inductive prior
decreases in data-rich regimes.

Only 5 epochs were run with a single random seed, so the reported results are
point estimates without confidence intervals.

The noise model is exclusively isotropic Gaussian
($\varepsilon \sim \mathcal{N}(0, \sigma^2 I)$). In real applications noise is
typically heteroscedastic, temporally correlated, sometimes non-Gaussian, and
often structural (sensor dropouts, missing values). Robustness measured here
does not automatically generalise to those noise forms.

Scalar $Q_t$, $R_t$ estimation assumes per-channel independent noise
components. Estimating a diagonal or full covariance matrix would yield a
richer noise model at the cost of a noticeably larger parameter count.

A broader hyperparameter-sensitivity study (hidden dimension, number of heads,
layers, kernel size, learning rate) was not performed. The configuration
presented is a reasonable default that is not guaranteed to be Pareto-optimal.

\section{Future Work}

A systematic sweep over $s \in \{1, 4, 8, 16, 32, 64\}$ would identify the
data-scarcity threshold at which the Kalman structure's regularising effect
first emerges. Replacing simulated Gaussian perturbations with sensor dropouts,
missing values, and outliers would bring the evaluation closer to real-world
deployment conditions. Introducing a meta-network that estimates $\sigma$ as a
trainable parameter would allow the model to adapt automatically to environments
with unknown noise levels. Replacing the scalar $Q_t$, $R_t$ with a diagonal
Cholesky factorisation would yield richer uncertainty estimates and potentially
improve calibration.
